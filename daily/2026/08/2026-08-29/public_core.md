## 2. Market And Event Delta

### Hormuz / crude
Seven commodity vessels crossed Hormuz in the preliminary Thursday count, including one VLGC; the count was below both the prior day's 17 and the 10-day average of 15. Brent settled Friday at $89.31/bbl (-0.43%) and WTI at $83.40/bbl (-0.16%); both declined for the week. **Analysis:** price action is discounting some probability of corridor improvement before physical normalization is established. A durable rise in vessel counts is therefore the critical falsifier/confirmation variable. [E01,E02]

### LNG / Europe
QatarEnergy's Edison force majeure now extends to early November. Five further cargoes scheduled from late September to early November were canceled, bringing the total to 29 cargoes / 3.8 bcm. Edison said 21 replacement cargoes, about 2 bcm, had already been sourced at Adriatic LNG. Germany simultaneously reported storage at 51.64% and improving injections, attributing this partly to a better winter-summer spread and weaker Asian LNG competition after the regional heatwave eased. **Analysis:** Europe is replacing disrupted term supply, but the marginal cargo balance has temporarily become less adversarial. [E03,E04]

### Refined products / Russia
Reuters sources put late-August Russian gasoline production near 80,000 t/d versus estimated summer demand of 115,000 t/d after outages at major refineries. Seaborne petroleum-product imports from Asia were expected around 270,000 tonnes in August and Belarus gasoline imports around 150,000 tonnes. **Analysis:** refinery outages are converting upstream/geopolitical risk into product-specific scarcity and import pull; monitor gasoline/diesel cracks and regional arbitrage rather than inferring easing from crude alone. [E05]
